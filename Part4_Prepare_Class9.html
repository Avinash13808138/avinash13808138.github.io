Part 5

5(a). Who writes comments, who reads them, and who never appears?

People who write comments choose to participate publicly. They might want to express an opinion, ask a question, criticize the video, or respond to someone else.

People who read comments include both commenters and silent readers. Your comment dataset does not identify everyone who read the discussion.

People missing from the dataset include:

  • Viewers who never comment.

  • People who never encounter the video.

  • People whose comments are unavailable when you collect.

  • People who avoid public participation.

The research issue is self-selection: participation is a choice, and that choice may relate to the opinions you want to study.

In your example, NBC News’s 12.2 million subscribers are not represented by the two comments you retrieved. Commenters need not be subscribers, and subscribers need not watch or comment.

Also, two comments do not necessarily mean two different people. One account can comment repeatedly.

Conclusion: Data captures observable comments from participating accounts on a selected video. It does not automatically represent the views of all viewers, subscribers, or the public.

5(b). What edits or shapes the comment section?

Here, “edits” includes processes that change which comments remain available or receive visibility, not just changes to their wording.

Process How it can affect your data
Channel moderation Comments may be removed or restricted
Held-for-review filters Some submitted comments may not become publicly visible
Author edits or deletions Text can change or disappear
Ranking Determines which comments appear first
Your collection limit Determines how far through that ordering you collect

The API supports ordering by time or relevance. Its documented default is time; your command did not explicitly request relevance ordering. Public requests also do not provide unrestricted access to held-for-review comments. developers.google.com

Example: If critical comments were disproportionately removed, the remaining discussion could appear more supportive. That illustrates a possible mechanism; we have no evidence that this happened in your NBC News collection.

Conclusion: Visible comments are shaped by participation, moderation, deletion, and ordering. They are not an untouched record of everything anyone attempted to say.

5(c). What selects the comments you can collect?

Your dataset results from several decisions:

Selection step What happened in your exercise
Channel You used the assigned NBC News channel
Video You selected the first video returned in its uploads list
Comment type You requested comment threads, rather than separately collecting all replies
Maximum number You requested up to 100 threads
Collection time You collected at a particular moment on November 1, 2026
Availability The API returned two threads at that moment

The assigned video-listing function uses the channel’s uploads playlist. Therefore, you did not use keyword-search ranking to choose the video in this exercise. Search ranking would matter in a design that first searched for videos. gojiplus.github.io

Why does the collection day matter?

A newly published video can accumulate comments rapidly. A collection shortly after publication may capture a different discussion from one conducted several days later.

Your two returned threads versus eleven reported comments illustrates why collection timing and measurement need attention. However, those numbers alone do not prove that new comments caused the difference.

Also, requesting 100 comments does not create a random sample of 100 viewers. It sets a retrieval limit, and your request returned only two threads.

Conclusion: Your sample depends on the selected channel, video, retrieval settings, available comments, and collection time. A larger collection would not automatically remove those selection issues.

5(d). What does the 30-day rule mean for reproducibility and your website?

For the public data in this exercise, the assigned policy requires refreshing or deleting stored data within 30 calendar days. Refreshing means updating the stored copy from current API data. developers.google.com

This creates a reproducibility challenge:

What you preserve What it enables
Code and package versions Repeating the computational procedure
Collection settings and timestamp Understanding how and when you produced the sample
A later, refreshed collection Analyzing currently available data, which may differ from the original

Someone can repeat your procedure but obtain different comments because the discussion has changed.

For your website, remember that comments printed inside an HTML page are also stored copies. Deleting the original CSV does not remove them from the page or repository.

Conclusion: Document the method carefully, manage stored copies, and acknowledge that exact recovery of the original dataset may not be possible.

“What is this comment section a sample of?”

For your exercise, the most defensible answer is the publicly retrievable comments on one selected NBC News video under your collection settings at a particular time. Any claim about a wider audience requires additional evidence and a justified sampling design.
